# Restaurant-manager personas

## Sam, the Short-Staffed Night Manager

Sam runs a small restaurant with a five-person team and personally moves between counter service, packing, and delivery coordination. He needs the delivery workflow to make important work hard to miss without demanding continuous attention.

### Goals

- Complete feasible delivery orders without interrupting service for customers at the counter. [I1, O1]
- Keep orders accurate while working with a small team. [I1, I5, O1]
- Resolve unavailable-item issues without losing control of the shift. [I5, O1]

### Behaviours

- Alternates between counter work, packing, and checking the shared tablet. [I1, O1]
- Uses handwritten order numbers to identify completed bags. [O1]
- Contacts customers when an ordered item is unavailable. [I5, O1]

### Pain points

- New orders can wait unnoticed while he serves walk-in customers. [I1, O1]
- Sold-out items remain orderable until he can return to the tablet. [I5]
- Finding customer contact information takes several steps. [O1]

### Context

- Manages five people in a small noodle shop. [I1]
- Experiences the heaviest workload from 18:00 to 20:30. [I1]
- Uses one shared delivery tablet at the counter. [I1, O1]

## Linh, the High-Volume Orchestrator

Linh manages a fast-casual restaurant where delivery represents a large share of dinner business. She values predictable flow, current preparation estimates, and controlled handovers.

### Goals

- Maintain predictable order throughput during the dinner peak. [I3, I6, O2]
- Reduce cancellations caused by unavailable items. [I3, O2]
- Ensure each completed order reaches the correct rider. [I6, O2]

### Behaviours

- Adjusts quoted preparation times as kitchen load changes. [I3]
- Stages completed bags by order number. [I6, O2]
- Assigns a staff member to monitor and relay tablet status. [O2]

### Pain points

- Items can remain orderable after they sell out. [I3, O2]
- Early riders repeatedly ask staff for updates. [O2]
- Similar customer names increase wrong-handover risk. [I6]

### Context

- Manages a high-volume fast-casual restaurant. [I3]
- Receives roughly half of dinner business through delivery. [I3]
- Has a dedicated staging process for completed bags. [I6, O2]

## Anh, the Floor-First Manager

Anh manages a full-service restaurant and protects the seated-guest experience while retaining delivery revenue. He needs delivery work to remain visible and coordinated without dominating the host stand.

### Goals

- Preserve a calm experience for seated guests. [I8]
- Keep riders informed without creating congestion at the entrance. [I8]
- Retain delivery revenue without allowing it to disrupt table service. [I8]

### Behaviours

- Prioritises seated guests when delivery and floor work compete. [I8]
- Relies on visual tablet information while managing the dining room. [I8]
- Coordinates rider waiting through the host stand. [I8]

### Pain points

- Repeated audible alerts disrupt the dining room. [I8]
- Waiting riders can crowd the host stand. [I8]
- Delivery activity competes with floor responsibilities for attention. [I8]

### Context

- Manages a full-service restaurant. [I8]
- Treats delivery as secondary but worthwhile revenue. [I8]
- Works where tablet sound is audible to seated guests. [I8]

## Distinction and selection

Sam is constrained mainly by limited attention, Linh by high-volume coordination, and Anh by conflict between delivery and the dining-room experience. **Sam is selected for Parts C–E** because observations show the clearest traceable need for low-attention order control and exception handling.
